📡 Getting Started

  1. Add nodes — tap ＋ (bottom right of the map) then tap the map, or right-click / long-press the map → "Add node here". Each node is a transmitter or receiver. ◎ centres the map on your location.
  2. Set antenna height — in the sidebar, edit each node's ANT HT (metres above ground). Tweak the colour swatch to recolour a node; the ▾ chevron collapses a card.
  3. Link nodes — tap ↔ then tap two nodes, or right-click a node marker → "Connect to …". Right-click a link to hide, isolate, or delete it. Each link becomes its own path; use + NEW PATH to chain several into a multi-hop route.
  4. Pick a band — open ⚙ SETTINGS and choose a radio preset (Meshtastic AU 915 MHz by default) or enter a custom frequency; TX/gain/RX/margin live here too.
  5. Analyse — click ▶ ANALYSE (bottom of the sidebar). Terrain is fetched along each link and the line-of-sight + 1st Fresnel zone are drawn below the map.
  6. Coverage — tick the COVERAGE box on a node and click COMPUTE, or ▣ COVERAGE at the bottom of the side panel for every ticked node. A terrain-aware coverage area is drawn in that node's colour.

✓ BUDGET OK — predicted signal meets the required margin in both directions. A short-dashed line means the budget closes but the direct path is blocked by terrain, so the signal relies on diffraction over it (long grey dashes = not yet analysed). ⚠ LOW MARGIN — predicted reception has less reserve than requested. ✕ BELOW SENSITIVITY — at least one direction is below sensitivity, or strict LOS excludes the path. Geometry (surface LOS/Fresnel) and observed reception are reported separately. These are estimates, not service guarantees.

HOW COVERAGE IS CALCULATED

Coverage and individual links use the same terrain, clutter and directional budget engine. Terrain is sampled at about 20 m, with a cap on long paths; individual marginal profiles refine to about 10 m. Coverage rays optionally adapt to large gaps and changing margins. The remote radio uses global settings and RX antenna height. Opaque fill means more predicted margin. Empty areas fail the selected model, not necessarily real-world reception. Results report actual sampling resolution.

SURFACE CLUTTER (OPTIONAL)

Optional surface clutter combines WorldCover class heights with measured canopy where available. Missing sources are reported. Geometric clutter adds diffraction and empirical through-clutter attenuation. Optional terminal correction uses representative clutter heights at both ends within its supported frequency range. Clearing radii default to zero and should describe verified clear space. Surveyed ground and local clutter heights can be set on each node.

SUN & SHADE (SOLAR EXPOSURE)

Right-click a node → ⓘ More info… to open the node panel. Alongside the facts and link list it scans the skyline in every direction (terrain out to 30 km, surface clutter out to 5 km) and traces the sun's daily arc across it — handy for siting a solar panel or checking when a mast clears the trees.

  • Panorama — the horizon as seen from the node: dark = terrain, coloured = canopy/buildings, the gold curve is the sun's path for the chosen date (hour ticks + sunrise/sunset marked). Drag sideways to pan the full 360°.
  • Sky dome — looking straight up: centre = overhead, rim = horizon. Green is sky blocked by the skyline, dark is open sky, the dot is the sun.
  • Status line — whether the node is in direct sun or shaded right now (and whether terrain or canopy is the culprit), plus the day's total: direct sun X of Y daylight (NN %). An under canopy warning shows if the eye point sits below the tree layer.
  • Controls — Date picks the day; the time slider scrubs minute-by-minute; Clutter toggles whether canopy/buildings count toward shading; Eye m is the observer height above ground (defaults to the antenna height — drop it to ~2 m to check a person or a roof panel).

Solar position uses the NOAA low-precision model (~0.01° accuracy); sunlight is treated as a straight ray (no atmospheric refraction), so times near the horizon are approximate.

Tips: your map is saved in this browser and restored next visit, and deletes can be undone from the message that appears. Drag the top edge of the terrain profile to resize it. On the profile, scroll or pinch to zoom, drag across a stretch to zoom to it, double-click (or ⤢ FULL) to reset; ⇔ FOLLOW MAP shows only the part of the link visible on the map. Terrain is AWS Open Terrain Tiles (~30 m, no key, cached in memory). Open ⋯ → Calculation log to see exactly what each calculation used — link budgets, clutter loading, and per-link results. Use ⇪ SHARE to copy a URL containing your full setup. Modelling is approximate (Bullington component, effective Earth radius and empirical clutter). Measurements and terrain imports are available under Settings → Measurements. See source resolution and height-sensitivity scenarios in each result. Use measured validation for engineered links.

About noise & sensitivity

Leave measured noise blank to estimate thermal noise + noise figure. Sensitivity remains editable for your hardware. Node overrides support separate radio and cable settings.

Propagation
About the model

Height scenarios show sensitivity, not a confidence interval. Bullington is the P.526 §4.5.1 component, not the full delta-Bullington/spherical-earth model. Predictions need field validation.

Surface clutter
About surface clutter

Surface clutter combines ESA WorldCover class heights with measured Meta/WRI canopy height where a canopy tile is published. Attenuation is per metre of clutter the ray passes through, scaled by frequency and capped per path. Results show which canopy source each link used.

Coverage 0 active
About coverage

Coverage is shown in each node's colour: more opaque means more predicted margin, fading out to the coverage edge, blank where the link won't close (e.g. terrain shadow). Adaptive rays refine wide sectors and changing margins, up to 720 rays; the result reports the spacing actually reached. The remote radio uses the global settings and RX antenna height. Run it with ▣ COVERAGE at the bottom of the side panel, or per node with COMPUTE.
